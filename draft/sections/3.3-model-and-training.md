Both trained conditions share the same backbone, DenseNet121 pretrained on a
multi-institution chest radiograph corpus through TorchXRayVision, using the
densenet121-res224-all weights trained on NIH ChestX-ray14, PadChest,
CheXpert, MIMIC-CXR, Open-I, and RSNA Pneumonia data. The backbone produces a
1024-dimensional pooled feature vector. A dropout layer at a rate of 0.2
precedes a single linear head mapping the 1024-dimensional feature to 51
logits, one per canonical finding, each followed by a sigmoid. There is no
separate coarse-group head: at any is-a edge, the parent probability is the
same 51-way sigmoid output used for that parent finding elsewhere. The full
model totals 7,018,309 parameters, of which 6,966,034 belong to the backbone
and 52,275 to the head, and requires 2.755 giga multiply-accumulate operations
per 224 by 224 image.

Three of the seven fine-tuning datasets described in subsection 3.1, namely
NIH ChestX-ray14, PadChest, and CheXpert, are also among the backbone's
pretraining sources. This is a dataset-level overlap rather than a
patient-level leakage: the patient-level stratified split guarantees no
patient crosses our own training, validation, and test partitions, but the
backbone had already been exposed to images drawn from these three source
datasets, under whatever splits TorchXRayVision's own pretraining used,
before fine-tuning began. The overlap does not affect the comparison between
the flat and hierarchical conditions, since both start from the identical
pretrained backbone, but it means the backbone's performance on these three
datasets should not be read as evidence of generalization to unseen data
sources the way its performance on TBX11K, VinDr-CXR, VinDr-PCXR, and
COVIDx-CXR4 can.

Two conditions were trained, otherwise identical in architecture, data, and
optimization schedule, differing only in loss function. The flat condition
minimizes the masked binary cross-entropy given in equation 1, writing z for
the raw logit the head produces for a given finding, sigma for the sigmoid
function applied to that logit, y for the binary target of the same finding,
and m for the applicability mask that is 1 when a finding is annotated for a
given image's source dataset and 0 otherwise; not-applicable findings
contribute to neither the numerator nor the denominator of the loss. The
hierarchical condition adds the differentiable consistency penalty given in
equation 2, following the Hierarchical Binary Cross-Entropy (HBCE) formulation
of Asadi and colleagues: for each is-a edge with parent g and child c, the
penalty term is the product of the rectified linear unit of the child
probability minus 0.5 and the rectified linear unit of 0.5 minus the parent
probability, a term that is positive only when the child is predicted
positive while its parent is predicted negative. Equation 3 gives the
hierarchical loss, this penalty summed over the 13 edges described in
subsection 3.2 and added to the flat masked binary cross-entropy loss with a
weighting factor of 0.5.

Both conditions are optimized with AdamW at a weight decay of 0.01, using
differential learning rates of 1e-5 for the backbone and 1e-4 for the head,
under a one-cycle cosine-annealing schedule with a 1,000-step linear warm-up
capped at 30 percent of total training steps. Training runs in bfloat16
mixed precision on a single graphics processing unit with a batch size of 32
and no gradient accumulation. Gradients are clipped to a maximum norm of 1.0.
The backbone is frozen for the first epoch only. Training runs for a maximum
of 15 epochs with early stopping on validation loss at a patience of 3
epochs; in practice neither condition triggered early stopping and both ran
the full 15 epochs. Four checkpoints are saved per run, selected respectively
by final epoch, lowest validation loss, highest validation macro area under
the receiver operating characteristic curve, and highest validation macro F1;
the checkpoint selected by highest validation macro area under the receiver
operating characteristic curve is the one used for all reported evaluation.
The random seed is fixed to 42 across the random, NumPy, PyTorch, and cuDNN
sources of randomness.

For the flat condition, the checkpoint used for evaluation was reached at
epoch 14 with a validation macro area under the receiver operating
characteristic curve of 0.6735, while the lowest validation loss of 0.1918
occurred at epoch 13. For the hierarchical condition, the evaluation
checkpoint was likewise reached at epoch 14 with a validation macro area under
the receiver operating characteristic curve of 0.6731, while the lowest
validation loss of 0.2032 occurred at epoch 15. The hierarchy-consistency
penalty at the final training epoch of the hierarchical condition was 0.0009,
confirming that the penalty term received a non-zero gradient signal during
training. The figure below plots training and validation loss and validation
