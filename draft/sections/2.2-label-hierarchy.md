The 51 canonical findings are drawn from Indonesian clinical authority: the
Indonesian Society of Respirology's general clinical practice guideline
published in 2021, the Ministry of Health's national clinical practice
guidelines for pneumonia, lung cancer, and tuberculosis, and the Indonesian
Doctor Competency Standard. The list represents the scope of thoracic findings
relevant to the target clinical deployment context and mixes clinical
diagnoses with conditions defined by a characteristic radiographic finding; it
is a flat list with no ranking or grouping implied by clinical authority
itself.

For the hierarchical training condition, we additionally define 13 is-a edges
over the 51 labels, grouping clinically related findings under a broader
parent: pneumonia is parent to COVID-19 pneumonia, aspiration pneumonia, and
other viral pneumonia; interstitial lung disease is parent to idiopathic
pulmonary fibrosis, cryptogenic organizing pneumonia, hypersensitivity
pneumonitis, silicosis, asbestosis, other pneumoconiosis, and sarcoidosis;
tuberculosis is parent to post-tuberculosis obstructive syndrome; pleural
effusion is parent to pleural empyema; and pulmonary hypertension is parent to
cor pulmonale. These edges are clinically motivated modelling choices used to
define the hierarchy-consistency-regularized loss, not an official taxonomy
published by any of the three authorities above. The model architecture stays
flat: there is no separate coarse-group head, and the parent probability at
any edge is read directly from that parent finding's own sigmoid output among
the 51. The figure below diagrams all 13 edges: five parent findings,
pneumonia, interstitial lung disease, tuberculosis, pleural effusion, and
pulmonary hypertension, together covering 18 of the 51 canonical findings.

A hierarchy defined this way is only as useful as the data available to
enforce it. Checked against the combined training corpus, only one of the 13
edges, pneumonia to COVID-19 pneumonia, has both its parent and its child
finding annotated with a meaningful number of positive examples in the same
image. Every other edge has at least one endpoint that is not-applicable for
every image currently in the corpus: pulmonary hypertension carries zero
annotated rows in the pooled corpus, and each of the 12 children under
interstitial lung disease, tuberculosis, pleural effusion, and the remaining
pneumonia subtypes other than COVID-19 pneumonia carries zero annotated rows.
Because the hierarchy-consistency penalty can only receive a gradient signal
on an edge where both parent and child are annotated for the same image, the
penalty in practice acts almost exclusively on the pneumonia to COVID-19
pneumonia edge for the current corpus, and this limits how much effect
hierarchical training can have on hierarchical consistency measured across all
13 edges. This is reported as a data coverage limitation of the current
corpus, not a limitation of the hierarchy-consistency-regularized loss itself.
