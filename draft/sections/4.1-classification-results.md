Table 1 reports overall discrimination, calibration, and selective-prediction
metrics for both conditions on the test partition of the pooled corpus, which
contains 43,696 test images across 27 signal-bearing findings. Validation and
test track closely for both conditions on every headline metric, for example
flat macro AUROC of 0.6735 on validation against 0.6954 on test, indicating
the stratified split is not producing overfitting between the two partitions;
validation numbers are omitted from the table below for brevity. Bold marks
the better value in each row, where lower is better for Expected Calibration
Error, the area under the risk-coverage curve, and the hierarchy violation
rate, and higher is better for every other metric.

The flat condition wins the majority of overall metrics and most per-label
AUROC and average precision rows, particularly on ranking metrics: AUROC,
mean average precision, and the Matthews correlation coefficient. The
hierarchical condition's wins cluster in specificity, in the hierarchy
violation rate, and in several rare findings drawn largely from PadChest,
including bronchiolitis, idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis, and hydropneumothorax
on validation. Calibration does not clearly favor the hierarchical condition
on this corpus: both the area under the risk-coverage curve and Expected
Calibration Error are slightly worse for the hierarchical condition than for
the flat condition, a reversal of an earlier finding observed before PadChest
was added to the pooled corpus. The reliability diagram below, left panel,
shows the hierarchical condition is more overconfident than the flat
condition in the middle confidence range, consistent with its higher
Expected Calibration Error; the risk-coverage curve, right panel, shows both
conditions trading risk for coverage almost identically until the
highest-confidence decile, where the hierarchical condition's risk rises
slightly faster.

The most reliably discriminated findings on the current corpus are COVID-19
pneumonia, tuberculosis, pulmonary edema, pleural effusion, and pneumothorax,
all reaching AUROC above 0.87 on test for at least one condition; these are
also the findings with the largest positive counts in the pooled corpus.
Findings with weak discrimination for both conditions, generally below 0.60
AUROC on test, are post-TB obstructive syndrome, airway foreign body,
pulmonary hypertension, bronchiectasis, and hydropneumothorax, all findings
with small positive counts inherited mostly from PadChest. Raw accuracy
figures, in the range of 0.94 to 0.96 macro and micro, are inflated by class
imbalance and are not informative on their own for this long-tailed,
27-label setting; the Matthews correlation coefficient, around 0.28 macro,
and balanced accuracy, around 0.55 macro, are the more honest headline
numbers for this corpus.
