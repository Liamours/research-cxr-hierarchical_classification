We pool seven public chest radiograph datasets: CheXpert, COVIDx-CXR4, NIH
ChestX-ray14, PadChest, TBX11K, VinDr-CXR, and VinDr-PCXR. Combined, these
datasets contribute 527,745 images to a single canonical comma-separated
values file, with each row carrying an image identifier, an image path, a
source-dataset tag, a split tag, and 51 label columns matching the canonical
disease list. Of the 51 canonical findings, 27 receive training signal from at
least one of the seven datasets; the remaining 24 findings stay not-applicable
for every image in the current corpus and are excluded from both training and
scoring until a dataset annotating them is added.

The combined corpus is split by multi-label iterative stratification following
Sechidis and colleagues, grouped at the patient or study level wherever a
patient or study identifier is recoverable, into 80 percent training, 10
percent validation, and 10 percent test partitions, using a fixed random seed
of 42. This yields 440,080 training images, 43,969 validation images, and
43,696 test images. No patient appears in more than one partition.

Each image is reduced to a single frontal view, preferring posteroanterior
over anteroposterior over other frontal projections, converted to grayscale,
and normalized to the TorchXRayVision pixel convention with values ranging
from negative 1024 to positive 1024. Images are resized so the shorter side
measures 256 pixels using bicubic interpolation, then center-cropped to 224 by
224 pixels. For sources distributed as DICOM, pixel values are rescaled using
the stored slope and intercept, inverted when the photometric interpretation
is MONOCHROME1, and clipped to the 0.5th and 99.5th percentiles before
scaling.

Labels marked uncertain in the source annotations, such as CheXpert's negative
one uncertain mention code, are mapped to negative following the U-Zero
convention used in prior chest radiograph classification work. Training-time
augmentation is applied only to the training partition and consists of a small
affine transform, specifically rotation up to 10 degrees, translation up to 5
percent of image size, and scaling within 0.95 to 1.05 of original size, along
with mild brightness and contrast jitter at a factor of 0.1. Horizontal
flipping is disabled throughout, because it would mirror the cardiac silhouette
and the aortic arch into an anatomically implausible configuration
corresponding to situs inversus, an anatomy present in only a small fraction of
the population.
