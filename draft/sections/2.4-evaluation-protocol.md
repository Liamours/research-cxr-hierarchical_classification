Discrimination is measured by area under the receiver operating characteristic
curve (AUROC) and mean average precision, each reported as a macro average
across the 27 signal-bearing findings and, for area under the receiver
operating characteristic curve, additionally as a micro average pooling all
predictions together. Threshold-based metrics, specifically F1, precision,
recall, specificity, accuracy, balanced accuracy, and the Matthews correlation
coefficient, are computed at a fixed decision threshold of 0.5 rather than a
threshold tuned per finding, and are likewise reported as macro, micro, or
weighted averages depending on the metric. All metrics respect the
applicability mask described in subsection 2.1: a finding not annotated for a
given image's source dataset does not enter that image's contribution to any
metric.

Calibration is measured by Expected Calibration Error using 15 equal-width
confidence bins. Selective prediction quality is measured by the area under
the risk-coverage curve (AURC), computed with the plug-in estimator of Zhou
and colleagues, where coverage is the fraction of predictions the model
commits to and risk is the error rate among committed predictions. We report
AURC flat, pooling every masked sample-finding pair into a single ranked list
before computing the estimator, and AURC macro, the unweighted mean of AURC
computed separately for each of the 27 signal-bearing findings. Both are
single-finding metrics; neither credits a coarse, parent-level prediction for
a true positive among its children, since that ancestor-rule scoring belongs
to the uncertainty-gated fallback mechanism described in the methodology and
is not part of the evaluation reported here.

Hierarchical consistency is measured as the hierarchy violation rate, the
fraction of positive child-finding predictions whose corresponding parent
finding is predicted negative, computed across the 13 is-a edges described in
subsection 2.2.

Localization is evaluated using Gradient-weighted Class Activation Mapping
(Grad-CAM) applied at the last convolutional block of the DenseNet121
backbone, against the National Institutes of Health ChestX-ray14 dataset's
official bounding-box release, which provides 984 boxes across 880 unique
images in eight categories. Six of the eight NIH categories map to canonical
findings in our label space: atelectasis, pleural effusion, cardiomegaly,
pneumonia, pneumothorax, and solitary pulmonary nodule; the infiltration
category has no corresponding canonical finding and is excluded. Bounding-box
images are cross-referenced against our own stratified re-split rather than
the NIH dataset's original split, yielding 118 usable boxes across 114 unique
images on the test partition after filtering to the six mapped findings. For
each ground-truth label on each image, a predicted bounding box is extracted
as the bounding box of the largest connected component whose Grad-CAM
activation exceeds the 90th percentile of that heatmap's own pixel values, a
percentile-based threshold chosen over a fixed fraction-of-maximum threshold
because the latter produced degenerate whole-image boxes on low-confidence
predictions. We report intersection over union, intersection over the
ground-truth box area, whether the heatmap's single peak pixel falls inside
the ground-truth box, and detection-style average precision at intersection
over union thresholds of 0.1, 0.25, and 0.5, with model confidence as the
detection score.
