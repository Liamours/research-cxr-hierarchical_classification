Table 1 reports overall discrimination, calibration, and selective-prediction
metrics for both conditions on the validation and test partitions of the
pooled corpus, which contains 43,969 validation images and 43,696 test images
across 27 signal-bearing findings. Bold marks the better value in each row,
where lower is better for Expected Calibration Error, the area under the
risk-coverage curve, and the hierarchy violation rate, and higher is better
for every other metric.

Validation and test track closely for both conditions on every headline
metric, for example flat macro AUROC of 0.6735 on validation against 0.6954
on test, indicating the stratified split is not producing overfitting between
the two partitions. The flat condition wins the majority of overall metrics
and most per-label AUROC and average precision rows, particularly on ranking
metrics: AUROC, mean average precision, and the Matthews correlation
coefficient. The hierarchical condition's wins cluster in specificity, in the
hierarchy violation rate, and in several rare findings drawn largely from
PadChest, including bronchiolitis, idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis, and
hydropneumothorax on validation. Calibration does not clearly favor the
hierarchical condition on this corpus: both the area under the risk-coverage
curve and Expected Calibration Error are slightly worse for the hierarchical
condition than for the flat condition, a reversal of an earlier finding
observed before PadChest was added to the pooled corpus.

The most reliably discriminated findings on the current corpus are COVID-19
pneumonia, tuberculosis, pulmonary edema, cardiomegaly, and pleural effusion,
all reaching AUROC above 0.84 on test for at least one condition; these are
also the findings with the largest positive counts in the pooled corpus.
Findings with weak discrimination for both conditions, generally below 0.60
AUROC on test, are hydropneumothorax, mediastinal tumor, lung cancer, and
airway foreign body, all findings with small positive counts inherited mostly
from PadChest. Raw accuracy figures, in the range of 0.94 to 0.96 macro and
micro, are inflated by class imbalance and are not informative on their own
for this long-tailed, 27-label setting; the Matthews correlation coefficient,
around 0.28 macro, and balanced accuracy, around 0.55 macro, are the more
honest headline numbers for this corpus.
