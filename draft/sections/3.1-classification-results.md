The flat condition wins the majority of overall metrics and most per-label
AUROC and average precision rows, particularly on ranking metrics: AUROC,
mean average precision, and the Matthews correlation coefficient. The
hierarchical condition's wins cluster in specificity, in the hierarchy
violation rate, and in several rare findings drawn largely from PadChest,
including bronchiolitis, idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis, and hydropneumothorax
on validation. Calibration does not clearly favor the hierarchical condition
on this corpus: both the area under the risk-coverage curve and Expected
Calibration Error are slightly worse for the hierarchical condition than for
the flat condition, a reversal of an earlier finding observed before PadChest
was added to the pooled corpus. The reliability diagram below shows the
hierarchical condition is more overconfident than the flat condition in the
middle confidence range, consistent with its higher Expected Calibration
Error; the risk-coverage curve shows both conditions trading risk for
coverage almost identically until the highest-confidence decile, where the
hierarchical condition's risk rises slightly faster.

The most reliably discriminated findings on the current corpus are COVID-19
pneumonia, tuberculosis, pulmonary edema, pleural effusion, and pneumothorax,
all reaching AUROC above 0.87 on test for at least one condition; these are
also the findings with the largest positive counts in the pooled corpus.
Findings with weak discrimination for both conditions, generally below 0.60
AUROC on test, are post-TB obstructive syndrome, airway foreign body,
pulmonary hypertension, bronchiectasis, and hydropneumothorax, all findings
with small positive counts inherited mostly from PadChest. Raw accuracy
figures, in the range of 0.94 to 0.96 macro and micro, are inflated by class
imbalance and are not informative on their own for this long-tailed,
27-label setting; the Matthews correlation coefficient, around 0.28 macro,
and balanced accuracy, around 0.55 macro, are the more honest headline
numbers for this corpus.

The figure below shows why three of these weak findings score so poorly,
plotting the empirical cumulative distribution of the flat model's predicted
probability on a log scale, separately for true positive and true negative
test images. For pulmonary hypertension the two distributions are nearly
identical, matching its near-random test AUROC of 0.5033. For bronchiectasis
the positive distribution sits only slightly to the right of the negative
one, consistent with its weak but non-random AUROC of 0.5412. Hydropneumothorax
has only three true positive test images, too few to support a distributional
claim, but its below-random AUROC of 0.3120 comes from those three cases
receiving lower predicted probability than a typical true negative rather
than higher, the opposite of the intended ranking.

The error patterns above are aggregate and statistical; the figure below
shows what a confident miss looks like on an individual image. For each of
the six findings with a bounding box annotation, described further in
subsection 3.2, it shows the flat model's single lowest predicted probability
among test images where that finding is truly present. Predicted
probabilities for these six cases range from 0.006 to 0.075, all far below
the 0.5 decision threshold despite every one of these images carrying the
finding the ground truth box marks. This is the same underlying behavior as
the low-AUROC findings above, made concrete on individual images rather than
read off a population-level curve.
